Defeater Cards: Characterizing and Managing Safety Assurance Case Defeaters

A Defeater Card is a structured documentation artifact for a defeater in a safety assurance case. It records the defeater's provenance, rationale, scope, temporal relevance, resolution and history. A card attaches to a node of the assurance case by its identifier and does not modify the notation.

Criteria R1–R3

Who identified and assessed the defeater, with what expertise, and a record of their inputs, so that the process can be reviewed independently.

Criteria R4–R5

Why the defeater matters, the assumptions it rests on, its severity, and the operating context and component it applies to.

Criteria R6–R10

How it is resolved and with what limitations, what triggers re-evaluation, the residual risk, and evidence that the resolution works.

Criteria R11–R14

The challenges and responses raised about the defeater, and how its resolution status was reached across reviews.

Criteria R15–R16
Background

What is a defeater?

A safety assurance case is a structured argument, supported by evidence, that a system meets its safety requirements. A defeater is a reason to doubt part of that argument. It may challenge a claim, the evidence offered for it, or the inference linking the two.

For example, the claim that a drone's detect-and-avoid subsystem identifies all nearby airborne hazards is challenged by the defeater "Unless sensor disagreement under partial occlusion or glare occurs, leading to false or missed detections."

Motivation

Why Defeater Cards?

No common reporting format
Defeaters are recorded in ad hoc ways, and assurance case notations such as GSN, CAE and eliminative argumentation offer no common format for reporting them.
Missing context for review
Existing defeater records often omit the context reviewers and auditors need: how a defeater was found, what it assumes, and why its resolution is considered adequate.
Re-evaluation as systems change
Assurance cases for evolving systems must be re-evaluated as the system or its operating context changes. Existing records rarely state what triggers each defeater or when it should be revisited.

See the paper, Section 2.

Design

Defeater Card structure

Cards are organized by the 5W1H framework (What, Why, Who, When, Where, How) plus a History section. Each field corresponds to one of sixteen documentation criteria (R1–R16) identified through a literature review. See Template for the full list.

    Integration

    Relation to assurance case notations

    Defeater Cards do not modify an assurance case notation. Each card references the node it concerns by identifier: the defeater node where the notation has one, and otherwise the challenged node. For a defeater that challenges an inference, the card references the link between the two nodes.

    Where a notation records structure or status natively, the notation is authoritative and the card refers to it. The card records information the notation does not hold: provenance, contributor expertise, re-evaluation triggers, residual risk, and the history of challenges and responses.
